Before You Buy a Tub-to-Shower Conversion Kit

There are essentially two options when switching out a tub with a shower: tiling a new shower or installing a shower enclosure kit, which is essentially a premade, ready-to-install shower enclosure. Installing a shower kit is often cheaper and easier than tiling a new shower, and the best shower kits on our list can look just as stylish as a tiled option. Choosing the best shower surrounds for a bathroom comes down to budget and preferred finished look.

When removing a bathtub, especially an old tub, there’s always the possibility of finding damage beneath. Since a new shower should last upward of a decade before the need to repair or update, it’s important to make sure the foundation it’s built on will last at least that amount of time as well.

Once the bathtub is pulled out, make sure to check the condition of the floor and subfloor, framing, and the existing plumbing. Look for signs of water damage and repair as needed. Repair any areas of subfloor or studs that might have been damaged in the removal process. It’s also a good idea to update the plumbing fixtures as part of the project to lower the chance of leaks in the future.

What to Consider When Choosing a Tub-to-Shower Conversion Kit

The best shower enclosures look beautiful and are easy to install, making it possible to convert tubs to showers on a budget. The best tub-to-shower conversion kits often include the wall panels and shower pan (though sometimes the pan is sold separately), and can include a shower door as well. When shopping for a tub-to-shower conversion kit, consider the shower’s size, shape and location, floor, installation process, and materials. Size

Size is one of the most important aspects to consider when choosing between the best showers for a bathroom renovation. The new shower needs to fit the space, so make sure to measure carefully.

Many standard alcove and drop-in tubs measure around 60 inches wide by 30 or 32 inches deep. When it comes to a shower, the National Kitchen & Bath Association recommends a minimum interior shower size of 36 inches wide by 36 inches deep. Small prefabricated showers can measure as little as 32 inches wide by 32 inches deep, and can go up in increments up to around 60 inches wide by 36 inches deep. It’s not too difficult to find shower kits to fit in an old tub space, though sometimes there might be a need to alter wall or flooring finishes to gain an extra few inches of width. Choose a shower the same footprint size as the bathtub for an easier renovation.

Corner tubs measure around 60 inches wide by 60 inches deep, so there is usually more than enough space to replace these large tubs with a stall or step-in shower. If installing a swinging shower door, make sure to account for clearance space. Shape and Location

Some of the most common bathtub types include alcove, drop-in, and corner bathtubs. The bathtub shape and location can determine the ideal shower kit, although it’s often possible to work around these factors with more renovation work. There are many shower stalls or walk-in showers that can fit into alcove or drop-in tub spaces. Corner shower kits are also common, making it fairly straightforward to replace a corner bathtub. Whether the shower is installed in the previous tub location or elsewhere in the bathroom, the plumbing fixtures and drain location may need to be modified.

The best tub-to-shower conversion kits can come in one piece or in several pieces to assemble on-site. While the best one-piece shower stall options are appealing, larger ones might not fit through the bathroom door. In this case, a multipiece shower kit is the right choice. Floor

Another consideration is the shower floor. Many of the best tub-to-shower conversion kits include a shower pan, often with a curb. As the name suggests, these have a raised curb and an appropriate slope to keep water contained and direct it down the drain.

Curbless showers are the other option. Since these don’t have a raised curb, they require a bit more renovation work. The bathroom flooring often needs to be altered to create a drainage slope and allow the shower pan to sit flush with the floor. While these pans are more difficult to install, curbless showers are more accessible and can make the bathroom look more open. Regardless of the shower floor type, look for a textured surface to help prevent slips and improve safety. Installation and Hardware

Shower kits come in alcove, corner, and freestanding options and can have rectangular, square, round, or angled configurations. Choose a design depending on the installation location and preferred look.

Installing one of the best shower inserts is much simpler than tiling a new shower, but that doesn’t mean it’s an easy task. When converting a tub to a shower, drain and water connections will likely need to be moved. Many shower kits have a center drain instead of a drain on one end (common for bathtubs). It might also be necessary to add a shower head or raise the height of the existing shower head to accommodate the new shower height.

Many homeowners call in professional installation experts to deal with these aspects, especially since some municipalities may require a permit and inspection to modify plumbing locations. Materials

Shower kits are usually made of acrylic, fiberglass, or composite materials. These materials are lightweight and durable, but each comes with its own pros and cons. Acrylic showers tend to be pricier than fiberglass showers, but they also tend to be more durable and last longer since the material is more scratch-, puncture-, and stain-resistant. Fiberglass is more susceptible to damage, including scratches and discoloration.

Q. What do I need to turn a tub into a shower?

Switching a tub for a shower is a multistep (and often multiday) process. You’ll need tools like a pry bar, hammer, or oscillating multi-tool to demolish the existing tub and surrounds. Remember to have the necessary eye and ear protection to do the job safely. Once the old tub is removed, basic plumbing and repair materials will likely be needed to fix any damage and set up the plumbing for the new shower. To install a new shower kit, you’ll need the tools and materials specified by the manufacturer for installation.

Q.Can I cut a tub to make it a walk-in shower?

Yes, with special tub cutouts, it is possible to cut out a portion of the tub’s side to make it similar to a walk-in shower. It won’t look as nice as a true walk-in shower, but it’s a budget-friendly option.

Q.Is it cheaper to tile a shower or buy a kit?

It’s almost always cheaper to buy and install a shower kit. Tiling a new shower is a pricey and lengthy process. While tiled showers are beautiful and worth the installation cost for many homeowners, those on a strict budget will find that a shower kit is often the economical choice. Q.Can I install a shower kit myself?

Yes, experienced DIYers can try to install a shower kit without professional help. It does take a wide range of know-how though, and only those comfortable with demolishing the old tub, updating the plumbing, and installing the shower kit should take on the job. Otherwise, hire a professional.
