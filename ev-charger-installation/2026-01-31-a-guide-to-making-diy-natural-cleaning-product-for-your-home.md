# A Guide to Making DIY Natural Cleaning Product for Your Home - This Old House

Skip to content iStock

DIY natural cleaning products are an eco-friendly and cost-effective alternative to store-bought cleaners. Using simple ingredients from your kitchen, you can create safe, functional cleaning solutions for your family and the environment. Natural cleaning products are nontoxic, sustainable, and better for your health—and for any children and pets in your home—because they don’t contain the harsh chemicals that many commercial cleaning products do while often performing just as well. This guide will teach you how to make your own natural cleaners, from all-purpose sprays to specialized formulas for every room in your home.

Essential Ingredients for Homemade Cleaners

Before diving into specific recipes, let’s explore the key ingredients you’ll need for your natural cleaning arsenal. These common household items form the foundation of most DIY cleaning solutions. Baking Soda

Baking soda is a versatile cleaning agent with mild abrasive properties. It’s excellent for scrubbing surfaces, deodorizing, and neutralizing odors. You can use baking soda on its own or combine it with other ingredients to create powerful cleaning pastes.

Pro Tip: Tom Silva, This Old House general contractor, describes baking soda’s cleaning power: “The baking soda is basically like a fine stone, so it can be a little aggressive. And the water is just getting into the stain itself, allowing everything to soak.” For tough stains on wood surfaces, he recommends mixing one part water with two parts baking soda to create a more aggressive cleaning paste.

Baking soda’s ability to neutralize both acids and bases helps maintain a balanced pH. This ensures that your surfaces are clean and less prone to damage over time. Castile Soap

Castile soap is a versatile, plant-based soap that you can use for various cleaning applications. It’s gentle but can effectively cut through dirt and grease, making it ideal for general cleaning tasks.

Pro Tip: Eric Hagerman, contributing editor at This Old House Magazine, recommends Dr. Bronner’s Pure-Castile Soap as a dirt-dissolving, biodegradable option that works on floors, in the laundry, “even on you! Try it in your next bath.”

Because it’s biodegradable, castile soap is an eco-friendly substitute for harsh detergents. You can use it safely even if your home has a gray water system as it doesn’t harm water sources. Lemons

Lemons are natural deodorizers and have antibacterial properties. Their citric acid content helps break down dirt and grime, while their fresh scent can make your home smell clean and inviting.

The natural oils in lemons uplift and refresh spaces and provide an extra layer of protection against bacteria. You can also incorporate lemons into more specialized cleaning solutions—like metal polish—due to their ability to dissolve mineral deposits.

While you might not associate olive oil with cleaning, it is excellent for polishing wood and stainless steel surfaces. It helps nourish wood and leaves a protective shine on metal appliances.

Olive oil can act as a natural conditioner for various surfaces, helping to restore and maintain their original luster. Its minimally abrasive nature ensures that it won’t scratch or damage surfaces, making it a gentle yet effective cleaning agent for delicate items. White Vinegar

White vinegar is a natural disinfectant and deodorizer. Its acidity makes it effective at cutting through grease and grime. Vinegar is particularly useful for cleaning glass, removing soap scum, and descaling appliances. The acetic acid in white vinegar also prevents mold and mildew buildup and improves your home’s air quality. iStock All-Purpose Cleaner Recipes

An all-purpose cleaner is a must-have in any cleaning kit. Here are two simple recipes that you can use throughout your home. Vinegar-Based All-Purpose Cleaner

This basic cleaner is easy to make and effective on most surfaces. Follow these steps to make it:

Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Shake well before each use.

Spray on surfaces and wipe clean with a microfiber cloth.

You can customize this vinegar-based solution by adding a few drops of essential oil, such as lavender or eucalyptus, for additional antibacterial properties and a pleasant aroma. Citrus-Infused All-Purpose Cleaner

Natural laundry solutions can be just as effective as commercial products. Try these recipes for clean, fresh-smelling clothes. Homemade Laundry Detergent

Create a natural, low-suds detergent using the following method:

Grate one bar of castile soap or use 1 cup of soap flakes.

Mix with 1 cup of washing soda and 1 cup of baking soda. Use 1–2 tablespoons per load.

Enhance this detergent by adding an optional half cup of borax, a natural mineral known for its potent cleaning properties. This can help brighten whites and tackle tougher stains with ease. Natural Stain Remover

For tough stains, try the following solution:

Mix a quarter cup of vinegar, a quarter cup of baking soda, and 1 tablespoon of castile soap in a spray bottle.

Fill with 2 cups of water and shake well.

Spray directly on stains before washing.

Presoak heavily stained fabrics in this mixture for 30 minutes before washing to loosen and lift even the most stubborn stains. Specialized Cleaning Formulas

Environmental Impact and Safety of Cleaning Products

One of the biggest advantages of DIY natural cleaning products is their positive impact on the environment and personal health. Nontoxic Alternatives

Natural cleaning products are safe to use around children and pets. Natural ingredients don’t release harmful fumes or leave toxic residues, reducing the risk of respiratory issues and skin irritations often caused by chemical cleaners. Reducing Plastic Waste

By making your own cleaners, you can significantly reduce the number of plastic bottles you use and discard. Reuse spray bottles and containers to minimize waste, and opt for glass containers and repurposed bottles to store your homemade solutions. This practice cuts down on plastic waste and ensures the longevity of your cleaning products, as glass is resistant to chemical reactions. Storage and Shelf Life of DIY Cleaners

You can store most homemade cleaners for several weeks to months. Here are some tips for proper storage:

Discard if you notice any changes in color, odor, or consistency.

Label containers with ingredients and creation date.

Shake well before each use as natural ingredients may separate.

Store in a cool, dark place away from direct sunlight.

Use clean, airtight containers or spray bottles.

Periodic checks and proper storage practices ensure that your homemade cleaners remain effective and safe to use over time. Always prioritize freshness to maintain your products’ cleaning efficacy. Our Conclusion
