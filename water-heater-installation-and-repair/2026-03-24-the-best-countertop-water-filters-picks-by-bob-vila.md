What to Consider When Buying a Countertop Water Filter

Before you can determine which is the best countertop water filter for your home, learn about the potential contaminants in your water supply and how best to eliminate them. Many companies offer free water analysis to help you pinpoint the specific compounds or particles in your water to filter from the tap water. Consider a few other characteristics when purchasing a countertop water filter to keep your family safe and hydrated. Types of Countertop Water Filters

The two types of countertop water filters are water filter pitchers and faucet filters. Understand the pros and cons of each when shopping for the best countertop water filter for your kitchen. Each kind of filter has unique characteristics for storage, filtration volume, and water decontamination. Water Filter Pitcher

Water filter pitchers help make water filtration easy and relatively affordable. They don’t need to be connected to the faucet and can be stored anywhere—on the counter or in the fridge. Additionally, they work well in office and dorm settings. Their only drawback is that they may need frequent refilling, depending on your household consumption. Replacing the filter monthly is typically recommended. Some pitchers display filter life to let you know when the filter is near the end of its life. To maintain your pitcher, wash it and all its components by hand every week. Faucet Filters

A countertop water filter system, which is also called a point-of-use water filtration system, attaches directly to the faucet, usually to the faucet’s aerator. Faucet filters are designed to save space while delivering on-demand filtered drinking water. They use filtration materials like activated carbon to eliminate contaminants, and they typically take minutes to install. Along with replacing the filter, faucet filters require some periodic maintenance. Size

The size of the water filter is proportional to its filtration capacity. To determine which size will work for your home’s needs, consider the number of people who will be drinking the water and the amount of countertop space you have in relation to the unit’s footprint. Also don’t forget that the water filter must be next to the sink. Filtration Ratings

Filtration capacity refers to the filter’s life: the number of gallons of water the filter can effectively process and the length of time the filter will last. Not all filters can clean the same contaminants, and some models may remove beneficial minerals, too. Some of the more common contaminants removed from water are chlorine, fluoride, and VOCs. Before purchasing a filter, have the water tested to find out which contaminants are present in your water supply and the ones that are most important to eliminate. Filter Maintenance Requirements

At some point, the filter on every water filter must be changed. The filter’s lifespan correlates directly to the model; every filter’s capabilities are different. Some have a short life, such as one or two months, while others last a long time, even a year or more. Before committing to a particular countertop water filter, consider the cost and frequency of replacing the filter to determine the overall cost of keeping the filter functioning effectively. When using a water filter, the taste of the water often changes subtly. Unchlorinated water tastes different from water directly from the tap. Some filters remove beneficial minerals from tap water, which could also alter the water’s taste and even change how the water feels. Look for a filtration system that either maintains or replaces essential minerals like magnesium and potassium. Certifications

Installing a countertop water filter is relatively easy. Here are a few common questions and answers regarding countertop water filter use.

Q. How does a countertop water filter work?

Water filters work by using a filtration mechanism to capture organisms and chemicals. A fabricated surface traps the larger contaminants, while a compound like activated carbon works as a magnet to trap smaller contaminants.

Q. How do you install a countertop water filter?

Use the hardware included with the filtration system to attach the filter to the faucet’s aerator. Though this may get in the way of the faucet’s water flow, it can provide enough water for drinking or cooking.

Q. Do countertop water filters work for all kitchen faucets?

Countertop water filters do not work with every type of kitchen faucet. Specifically, more modern designs and detachable faucets with spray nozzles may be incompatible with countertop water filters.

Q. What common contaminants does a countertop water filter remove?

Most filters can remove chlorine, fluoride, bacteria, and parasites. To know exactly which contaminants a specific filter can remove or reduce, consult the manufacturer’s information regarding filter certification.

Q. Can bacteria grow in my countertop water filter?
