If you're struggling with your Windows PC using too much power or your laptop battery dying, you may need to change your power and sleep settings. In this article, we'll cover how to change your power and sleep settings in Windows via the Settings app and the Control Panel. We'll also outline the best power settings to conserve as much energy as possible when using your laptop or desktop PC. Ways to Change Power and Sleep Settings

To change your power and sleep settings in Windows, go to Settings > System > Power. You can change your power mode, choose how long it takes for your computer to go to sleep, and more. You can also edit power settings in Control Panel by opening Power Options. There are more power plan options in the Control Panel. Steps Section 1 of 3: Settings App Download Article 1

Open the Settings app. The easiest way to do this is to press ⊞ Win+I, but you can also right-click the Windows icon in the taskbar and choose Settings from the Power User menu.

The steps in this section also apply to Windows 10, but the menu items may be in a different location. 2

Click System on the left-hand side. It's the second option from the top, and it has an icon of a computer screen. Advertisement 3

Click Power. It's about halfway down the window, and it has the power icon on it. 4

Select Energy recommendations (optional). To reduce your computer's energy use, you can use the built-in energy-saving recommendations in this menu. This menu gives you an easy way to access a variety of power settings, though you can access these options in the other menus, as well.

To apply any of these options, just click the Apply button, and the settings will be changed for you. 5

To save the most battery on your laptop, choose Best Power Efficiency. This setting limits background activity and reduces power consumption whether your computer is plugged in or not.

If your computer feels slow in Best Power Efficiency mode, you can change it to Best Performance. However, this will use more power.

Balanced is a happy medium between the two options, meaning you won't sacrifice much performance while still saving some energy.

If you're using a desktop computer instead of a laptop, you'll only see the option for "Plugged in." 6

Choose how long your computer must idle before it sleeps or hibernates. You'll be able to choose your options for when your computer is plugged in and when it's on battery (unless you're using a desktop, which only has the "plugged in" options).[2]XTrustworthy SourceMicrosoft SupportTechnical support and product information from Microsoft.Go to source

Turn my screen off after: To save energy, your computer can turn your screen off after a set amount of time. Your computer will stay on, but the screen will simply go dark.

Make my device sleep after: You can also set your computer to sleep after a certain amount of time. This will save more energy than turning your screen off.

Make my device hibernate after: Just like the other options, you can tell your computer to hibernate after a certain amount of time. Hibernation uses zero power, so it is the most energy-efficient option.

What's the difference between sleep and hibernation? When you put your computer to sleep, it stays on, but your session is saved to RAM to use less power. When you put your computer into hibernation, it turns off and saves your session to your hard drive. Hibernation uses zero power.[3]XResearch source

Hibernation is better if you're stepping away from your computer for a long time, and sleep is best if you're just taking a short break. 7

Enable energy saver. Energy saver is a new Windows 11 feature that replaces older battery saver modes. To enable it, click on Energy saver and toggle on "Always use energy saver." When energy saver is on, you'll notice the following changes:

You can't change your power mode settings.

Your display brightness will be reduced by 30%, but this setting can be disabled.

Transparency effects on windows are disabled.

Non-critical Windows updates are blocked. Most telemetry is blocked.

Most tasks in Task Scheduler won't run.[4]XResearch source 8

Change what the power buttons do. Expand Power & sleep button controls. You can then change what your power and sleep buttons do while plugged in and while on battery.

If you're on a desktop, you'll only see the options for "plugged in." 9

Other power-saving options. There are more power-saving options in Windows Settings that aren't in the power menu. Here are some other options you can change:[5]XTrustworthy SourceMicrosoft SupportTechnical support and product information from Microsoft.Go to source

Enable dark mode: If you have an OLED screen, enabling dark mode can reduce power consumption. Note that if you have any other type of screen (such as an LCD), this won't save any energy. Go to Personalization > Colors.

Click the dropdown next to Choose your mode. Select Dark.

Disable Wi-Fi and Bluetooth: If you're not using Wi-Fi and/or Bluetooth, you can disable them to save some power.

Click the Wi-Fi icon in the system tray (in the bottom-right corner of the screen).

Click the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth buttons to disable them. Alternatively, you can toggle on airplane mode in this menu to disable Wi-Fi and Bluetooth in one click without having to turn them off.

Enable dynamic refresh rate: When your computer screen isn't moving (like when you're reading text), dynamic refresh rate will lower your screen's refresh rate and save some energy. Once you move your mouse, the refresh rate will return to its normal rate.

Go to System > Display > Advanced display.

Toggle on Dynamic refresh rate. Note that not all monitors support dynamic refresh rate, so if the toggle is grayed out, you won't be able to enable it. Advertisement Section 2 of 3: Control Panel Download Article 1

Open the Control Panel. To do this, type control panel into the taskbar search bar, then click Control Panel in the Start menu that pops up. 2

Click Power Options. If you're in icon view, it will be listed alphabetically. If you're in category view, click System and Security > Power Options. 3

Choose a power plan. The Control Panel gives you more power plan options than Windows Settings. At the top, you'll see recommended plans; if you click the arrow next to "Show additional plans," more will appear.

Balanced: This is Windows' recommended plan. This option will balance performance and energy consumption.

Power saver: This option reduces energy consumption to minimize power use, which may affect your PC's performance.

High performance: This option favors performance at the cost of energy consumption.

Ultimate Performance: This option uses the most power and is best used on high-end PCs.

To customize a power plan, click Change plan settings next to the one you want to edit. You can choose how long it takes to turn off the display and how long it takes for the computer to go to sleep. You can also edit advanced power settings by clicking Change advanced power settings.

Alternatively, you can click Choose when to turn off the display or Change when the computer sleeps in the left-hand sidebar to change your power plan settings. 4

Change what the power buttons do. To do this, click Change what the power buttons do in the left-hand sidebar. This will take you to a menu where you can change what happens when you press the power and sleep buttons. After you make your changes, click Save changes to return to the previous screen.

If you want to change your shutdown settings, click Change settings that are currently unavailable at the top of the window. You can then enable or disable fast startup, choose whether sleep and hibernate appear in the Power menu, and choose whether the lock option appears in the account picture menu. 5

Create your own power plan. If none of the existing power plans fit your needs, you can create your own. Click Create a power plan in the left-hand sidebar to continue. Choose a power plan to use as a base.

Give your power plan a name, then click Next.

Choose how long it takes before the display turns off.

Choose how long it takes before your computer goes to sleep.

Click Create to make your power plan. You can now select it from the power plan menu. Advertisement Section 3 of 3: Best Power & Sleep Settings in Windows Download Article

According to Microsoft, these are the best settings to reduce power consumption in Windows:[6]XTrustworthy SourceMicrosoft SupportTechnical support and product information from Microsoft.Go to source

Power mode: Choose Best Power Efficiency to reduce background activity, which lowers power consumption.

Location:Settings > System > Power & battery > Power Mode

Energy saver: Toggle this option on, and enable Lower screen brightness when using energy saver. This feature is designed to lower the amount of power your computer uses.

Location:Settings > System > Power & battery > Energy saver

Screen & sleep timers: Set the shortest feasible time to turn off your screen and put your computer to sleep.

[Settings in Windows](https://settings-in-windows.curblist.xyz/settings-in-windows/20260826.html)

Location:Settings > System > Power & battery > Screen, sleep & hibernate timeouts

Dark mode: If you have an OLED monitor, turning on dark mode will reduce power consumption. If you don't have an OLED monitor (such as an LCD screen), this option won't do anything to help power consumption.

Location:Settings > Personalization > Colors > Choose your mode

Wi-Fi/Bluetooth: Disabling these options when you're not using them will lower your computer's overall power consumption.

Location: Click the Wi-Fi icon in your system tray, then click the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth buttons to toggle them off.

Dynamic refresh rate: When your computer's screen isn't moving, this feature will slow your monitor's refresh rate down, which saves power.
