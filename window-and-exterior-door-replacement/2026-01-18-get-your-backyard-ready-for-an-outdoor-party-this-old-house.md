# Get Your Backyard Ready for an Outdoor Party - This Old House

Skip to content iStock

After a long winter and messy spring, your backyard may need some attention before hosting guests in the summer. Organizing these tasks may feel overwhelming, especially if your space has been neglected. This guide will walk you through the steps to get your backyard party-ready, from lawn care to setting the atmosphere. Whether planning a small gathering or a large celebration, these tips will help you make your outdoor space inviting and functional for you and your guests. How To Prepare for an Outdoor Party

Start by evaluating your backyard and listing areas that need attention. Prioritize your tasks based on the size of your guest list and the type of party you’re hosting. 1. Fix lawn problems iStock

The first impression guests will have of your outdoor space is your lawn. Address any noticeable issues before the event to create a welcoming atmosphere. Common lawn problems include bare patches, weeds, and uneven growth. Here are some quick fixes:

Overseed bare spots with grass seed and cover with a thin layer of topsoil

[Ready for an](https://ready-for-an.themaplelane.com/ready-for-an/20260822.html)

Remove weeds by hand or use a selective herbicide

Mow the lawn at the proper height for your grass type to promote even growth

Pro Tip: This Old House landscape contractor Roger Cook advises: “Set mower blades 3 inches high, because tall grass grows deep roots and shades out weeds; keep blades sharp so the grass is cut rather than ripped.” He also recommends mulching mowers, which leave chopped clippings on the lawn to turn into compost, feeding the lawn naturally.

You can find more detailed advice for livening your lawn below: How To Mow a Lawn How To Fertilize a Lawn How To Maintain a Lawn Organically 3. Prune shrubs and flowers

Pruning improves the appearance of your plants and promotes healthy growth. Remove dead, damaged, or diseased branches and shape your shrubs to keep them from obstructing walkways or seating areas. For flowering plants, deadhead old blooms to encourage new growth and more flowers.

Learn more about proper pruning techniques by reading these shrub pruning dos and don’ts.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook advises restraint when pruning: “I don’t take off more than a third of the shrub’s branches in any year.” You can always come back and prune again, but overdoing it can send a plant into shock right before your party. 4. Decorate with plants in containers iStock

Container gardening is a neat way to add color and give your garden an interesting look. Choose plants that fit in with your existing landscaping and consider the following tips:

Use a variety of container sizes and styles for visual appeal

Group plants with similar water and sunlight needs together

Include a mix of flowering plants, foliage plants, and trailing plants for dimension

5. Polish up driveway and walkway surfaces

Hardscaping often looks unsightly after a season of salt and shoveling. Clean and well-maintained hardscaping creates a polished look for your outdoor space. Fix any winter damage to keep your driveway and walkways safe and in good shape:

Pressure wash concrete surfaces to remove dirt and stains

Fill cracks in concrete or asphalt to prevent further damage

Reapply sealant to protect surfaces from future wear and tear

Battery-operated LED lights for flexibility and easy installation

A great party requires great ambiance. Use strategic lighting types and placement to create a cozy atmosphere:

String lights hung overhead for a magical effect

Lanterns placed on tables or hung from trees

Candles in hurricane glasses for a soft, flickering glow

Use outdoor-rated lighting products and follow proper safety precautions when using open flames.

Pro Tip: For a showstopping effect, try floating luminaries. Tie clear fishing line (25-pound nylon tippet works well) around the wire handle of small glass lanterns, then suspend them from a low-hanging tree branch at staggered heights. Place a few drops of water under each votive candle to ensure easy cleanup later—the melted wax will pop right out when cool. Or just use LED votives for a worry-free glow that lasts all night. 9. Pro Tip: This Old House’s Tom Silva recommends skipping the power washer when prepping your deck for a new finish. “I don’t like a power washer because a lot of people use them and they’re too powerful. They actually damage the wood fiber,” he says. Instead, use a pump sprayer with a biodegradable wood cleaner mixed with water on a 1-to-1 ratio. Spray the whole deck, wait about 10 minutes, then scrub it off with a brush. 10. Clean the patio

Moss and mildew can make a patio slippery, while debris can leave behind stains. Here’s how to address common patio problems:

Use a pressure washer or stiff brush to remove dirt and debris

Apply a moss and algae remover to prevent slippery surfaces

Treat oil or grease stains with a degreaser designed for your patio material

Use a specialized brick cleaner for stubborn stains on brick patios. To prevent moss growth, make sure the patio drains thoroughly. Trim nearby vegetation to increase sunlight exposure. 11. Get your grill ready

Check gas connections and hoses for leaks (if using a gas grill)
