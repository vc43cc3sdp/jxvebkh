Table saws are powerful tools that can significantly improve efficiency in woodworking projects. But they also pose serious safety risks if you don’t use them properly. Each year, tens of thousands of table saw injuries happen—often because the user removed or didn’t use certain safety devices designed to keep them safe.

In this guide, we’ll explore basic table saw safety measures to help you avoid accidents and operate your saw with confidence. Ask This Old House general contractor Tom Silva shares various table saw safety techniques and features in the video above. Table Saw Risks

Table saws can cause severe injuries, from lacerations and amputations to deaths. The primary dangers associated with table saws include:

Contact with the blade: Accidental contact with the spinning blade can lead to severe cuts or amputations.

Flying debris: Wood chips and sawdust can injure your eyes without proper protection.

Kickback: When the blade catches the workpiece and throws it back toward you. Precautionary Measures for Table Saws

To reduce the risks associated with table saw operation, follow these safety precautions. Your Environment and Positioning

Before you even think about turning on a table saw, one of the most important steps is to check your surroundings. Make sure your space is clear and recognize the kickback zones around the saw.

Always stand to the side of the saw blade rather than directly in line with it. This positioning helps you stay clear of the path of a potential kickback. The Right Safety Gear

Tom Silva, This Old House general contractor, puts the stakes in perspective: “In over 35 years, no one on my sites has lost a finger, broken a bone, or put out an eye.” But as he’s quick to point out, that record isn’t luck—it’s the result of never stopping to think about the safest way to get things done. “You have to imagine what could happen, and protect yourself against it.”

Wearing the right safety gear is your first line of defense against table saw injuries. Make sure you have the following equipment ready for use:

Ear protection: Shield your hearing from the loud noise of the saw.

Dust collection system: Reduce airborne sawdust and improve visibility.

Featherboards: Help guide your workpiece and prevent kickback.

Push sticks: Keep your hands away from the blade when making narrow cuts.

Safety glasses: Protect your eyes from flying debris and sawdust.

Pro Tip: Tom Silva, This Old House general contractor, stresses the importance of choosing gear that won’t slow you down: “When it comes to protective equipment, I choose what fits and allows me to move easily.” Besides fit, check the performance rating—look for an ANSI (American National Standards Institute) rating on safety glasses and other gear to ensure they meet recognized protection standards. Mental Focus and Maintenance

Operating a table saw requires your full attention. Distractions, fatigue, or even an off day at work can increase the risk of an accident.

Regular maintenance and inspection of the table saw also mean that all safety features and mechanical components are functioning correctly. Check the alignment of the blade, fence, and miter gauge often to prevent any unexpected movements that can lead to dangerous situations.

To check your table saw’s alignment, measure from the miter slot to both the front and back edge of the blade, then repeat from the miter slot to the front and back of the fence. According to This Old House Magazine, each pair of measurements should be the same. But even a properly adjusted saw can kick back if the blade is dull or it’s pinched by unstable wood—so for safety’s sake, always use the saw’s splitter and antikickback pawls, and never stand directly behind the blade when ripping wood. Setting Up Your Table Saw

Follow these steps to make sure your saw is ready for safe use:

Install the riving knife: This metal piece helps prevent kickback by keeping the cut portion of the board from pinching the blade.

Adjust the blade height: Set the blade so that it extends less than a quarter-inch above the thickness of the material you’re cutting.

Align the fence: Check that the fence is parallel to the blade to prevent binding and potential kickback.

Check the miter gauge: Verify that it moves smoothly in its slot and is square to the blade.

Safe Operating Techniques for Table Saws

Using the right techniques can help you avoid accidents. Here are some more tips:

Let the blade come to a complete stop before reaching for the cut pieces.

Avoid wearing loose clothing, jewelry, or gloves that could get caught in the blade.

Keep your work area clean and free of clutter to prevent tripping hazards.

Never reach over or behind the blade while it’s spinning.

Use push sticks for narrow cuts to keep your hands away from the blade.

Understanding and Using Safety Features for Table Saws

Modern table saws have built-in safety features to prevent accidents and minimize injuries. Understand and properly use these features:

Anti-kickback pawls: These are small, spring-loaded devices with teeth that allow the workpiece to move forward but dig into the wood if it tries to move backward, preventing kickback.

Blade guard: The blade guard is a clear plastic cover that sits over the blade, protecting you from accidental contact and flying debris. Many users remove this guard for convenience, but it’s an important safety feature that should always be in place when possible.

Riving knife: As mentioned earlier, the riving knife is a curved metal plate that sits behind the blade and moves up and down with it. It helps prevent kickback by keeping the cut portion of the board from pinching the blade.

Advanced Safety Technology for Table Saws

Some table saws now include advanced safety technologies to further reduce the risk of injuries. One such innovation is flesh-sensing technology, which Silva demonstrates.

This system uses an electrical current to detect contact with human skin. If the blade comes into contact with flesh, it triggers a brake that stops the blade within milliseconds and drops it below the table surface.

While this technology can save your fingers and hands from severe injury, it’s important to note that it shouldn’t replace proper safety practices and techniques. Maintaining Your Table Saw
