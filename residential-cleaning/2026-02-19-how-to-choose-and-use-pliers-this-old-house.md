Pliers are essential tools for any homeowner or professional tradesperson. They can grip, twist, cut, and pull, making them indispensable for a wide range of tasks. In this guide, we’ll explore the various types of pliers available and how to use them. In the video above, This Old House plumbing and heating expert Richard Trethewey shows the proper techniques for selecting and using different pliers. Types of Pliers

Understanding the different types of pliers helps you pick the right tool for your job. Here are some common varieties. Slip-Joint Pliers

Slip-joint pliers are versatile tools suitable for light-duty tasks. They feature an adjustable pivot point that allows the jaws to open wider to fit various sizes of nuts, bolts, and pipes. These pliers are ideal for: Gripping small to medium-sized objects Light twisting and turning tasks General household repairs

Pro Tip: Richard Trethewey points out that even the best pliers have limits. When a nut is really seized, he recommends switching from pliers to an adjustable open-end wrench: “Now, with that, I can set it and tighten it down on the nut. And now all of my effort, all of my force is to tighten that nut and none of it is trying to hold those handles together.” He also notes that locking pliers bridge the gap—once they lock on, all your effort goes to turning rather than squeezing. Tongue-and-Groove Pliers

Also known as channel-lock pliers, tongue-and-groove pliers offer greater holding power and are easier to adjust than slip-joint pliers. They have a unique channel design that allows for multiple jaw positions, making them suitable for many object sizes. Key features include the following: Multiple adjustment positions Strong gripping power

Ideal for plumbing tasks and larger objects

Pro Tip: As covered in This Old House Magazine, pliers fall into three basic categories: locking, adjustable, and nonadjustable. Locking pliers tighten mechanically onto the workpiece, freeing you to pull, twist, or even let go without losing your grip. Adjustable pliers can be set wider or narrower yet stay parallel at any size opening once the handles are engaged. Nonadjustable pliers open and close around a fixed, flat joint—these include needlenose and lineman’s pliers. Proper Techniques for Using Pliers

To get the most out of your pliers, you should employ proper techniques. Here are some key tips to keep in mind. Gripping and Turning

When using pliers to grip and turn objects, proper orientation is key. First, position the pliers so that when you swing the handle, the top jaw follows the lower jaw. Avoid orienting the pliers in the opposite direction, as this will make the jaws open and lose their grip. For maximum grip, slide the pliers all the way onto the nut or object to create a three-corner bite.

When using pliers for cutting tasks, choose the appropriate type of pliers for the material you’re cutting (e.g., diagonal cutters for wire and end nippers for nails). Then, position the cutting edges as close to the joint as possible for maximum leverage. Apply steady pressure to make a clean cut without damaging the surrounding area. Specialized Pliers for Specific Tasks

While general-purpose pliers can handle many jobs, some tasks require specialized tools. Here are a few examples. Locking Pliers

You can adjust locking pliers, also known as vise-grips, to automatically clamp onto a workpiece. This feature removes the need for constant hand pressure, making them ideal for the following:

Holding pieces together during welding or gluing Removing stuck or stripped screws

Clamping materials in place for cutting or drilling End Nippers

End nippers are made for pulling and cutting nails. Their unique jaw design allows you to securely grip nail heads, cut nails flush with surfaces, and remove embedded nails with minimal damage to surrounding materials. Fencing Pliers

These multi-purpose tools are designed for working with wire fencing but can be useful in various applications. Fencing pliers typically include features for gripping and twisting wire, cutting wire and small nails, hammering staples, and pulling staples or nails.

Pro Tip: As Richard explained during a pliers overview on the show, fencing pliers are a standout example of industry-specific design: “This is for the fencing industry. It combines a hammer for the staple and this can pull the wire and the cutter to cut the wire ties.” Groove Joint Pliers

Groove joint or water pump pliers are versatile with their adjustable size settings, making them a go-to tool for plumbing and other tasks where you need to grip various sizes of nuts and pipes.

These pliers can be particularly useful when transitioning between different nuts or bolts on the same project. Trethewey showcases in the video how groove joint pliers can quickly adjust to different widths, saving time and effort on the job. Pipe Wrench

A pipe wrench, also known as a Stilson, is best for plumbing tasks involving round pipes. Its unique design allows for a firm grip on cylindrical objects. Trethewey highlights the importance of adjusting the wrench to touch the pipe in three places‌—a technique known as the three-corner bite‌—so the pipe doesn’t deform during work. This method prevents leaks and helps get a proper fit. Safety Considerations When Using Pliers

When working with pliers, always prioritize safety. Keep these precautions in mind:

Wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from flying debris.

Use insulated pliers when working with electrical components, and make sure the power is off.

Avoid using pliers as hammers or pry bars, as this can damage the tool and potentially cause injury.

Keep your workspace clean and organized to prevent accidents.

Always use the correct plier type for the task to avoid damaging the tool or workpiece.
