Revamping your bathroom doesn’t always require a complete overhaul. With some creativity and know-how, you can transform your space into a stylish oasis without breaking the bank. Here, we explore 25 thrifty ways to redesign your bathroom, from simple updates to more involved projects. 1. Revitalize Your Claw-Foot Tub

An old claw-foot tub can be given new life with a simple refresh. Instead of replacing this timeless fixture, consider reglazing it to save money and preserve its classic charm. You can turn your tub into a striking focal point by painting the exterior a bold or vibrant hue. Alternatively, create a soft contrast by keeping the interior white and choosing a muted color for the outside. This saves you from buying a new tub and allows you to maintain your bathroom’s vintage appeal.

In a real-world example featured in This Old House Magazine, homeowners Jackie and Matt Metropoulos hired a Miracle Method technician to resurface the old cast-iron bathtub in their 1919 house. The tub had a green water mark under the faucet, several nasty dings on the lip, and a dull, gritty texture from years of improper cleaning. After a day’s work—including acid-etching, filling chips with polyester resin, and spraying three coats of acrylic urethane enamel—the fixture looked brand new. As TOH plumbing and heating expert Richard Trethewey put it: “Professional refinishing can be a cost-efficient solution for that retro look.”

Pro Tip: Chris Beer, a tub refinishing specialist featured on Ask This Old House, says vintage claw-foots are well worth the effort: “They’re nearly 100 years old and they’re made way better than the repros, and you can bring them right back to make them look like new.” He even applies custom colors—like a shade called “Shadow”—using a customer-supplied paint code for a truly personalized look. 2. Add Some Fun Wallpaper

Wallpaper is a great way to introduce color and pattern to your bathroom without a major renovation. When selecting wallpaper for this humid environment, opt for coated varieties that are easy to clean and can withstand moisture. If you’re feeling creative, you could make your own graphic wall covering using inexpensive peel-and-stick designs. These removable options allow you to experiment with different styles without committing to a permanent change, making them perfect for renters or those who like to update their decor frequently. Photo by Ken Gutmaker

Pro Tip: When homeowner Sean Lewis moved into a 1920s rowhouse, he initially balked at the bathroom’s peachy-pink and burgundy Art Deco tile—but eventually fell in love with it. To make the vintage tile look intentional during a renovation, Lewis chose a modern floral wallpaper from Rifle Paper Co. that echoed the tile’s colors for the upper half of the walls. He and his mom hung the paper themselves, then painted the stark white ceiling a warm peach tone to pull the whole room together—proof that wallpaper can bridge old and new on any budget. 3. Use Faux Candles

Create a spa-like ambiance in your bathroom with the soft glow of candles, minus the fire hazard. Battery-powered LED wax pillars offer a safe alternative to traditional candles while still providing a warm, flickering light. To enhance the effect, install salvaged wall candle sconces to hold these faux candles. Look for sconces at flea markets or antique stores for unique, budget-friendly options that can be easily mounted on your bathroom walls. 4. Try a New Towel Rack

A flea-market coat rack can serve as an attractive and practical towel holder. Before installation, be sure to seal its aged finish with clear polyurethane to protect it from bathroom humidity. For a more traditional look, consider installing a row of Shaker-style pegs. Choose pegs in a finish that complements your bathroom’s existing hardware for a cohesive look. Photo by Dominic Blackmore/IPC Images 5. Add a Tub Surround

Add a custom surround to give your drop-in tub a vintage makeover. For a wood surround that can withstand bathroom moisture, build the deck using plywood topped with waterproof laminate. Cap the edges with solid wood stock for a finished look. Use exterior-grade beadboard plywood for the sides and add baseboard molding at the bottom for a polished appearance.

For a more rustic and budget-friendly option, consider crafting a surround from corrugated, galvanized steel roof paneling. This material is not only affordable but also naturally resistant to rust and moisture. You can find all the necessary materials for either option at home improvement centers for less than $100, making this a cost-effective way to dramatically change the look of your bathtub. Opt for flush-mount mirrors instead of recessed medicine cabinets to avoid costly wall modifications. These easy-to-hang mirrors offer flexibility in design and can be easily swapped out to update your bathroom’s style. Scout resale shops and flea markets for unique mirror frames that complement your decor. Create a striking accent wall using subway tiles to make a big impact in your bathroom. Extending the tiles from floor to ceiling can visually elongate the room, making it appear taller. Subway tiles come in a variety of colors and finishes, allowing you to customize the look to fit your style. With prices starting as low as 99 cents per square foot at home improvement stores, this is an affordable way to add a classic, timeless element to your bathroom design. Photo by Caroline Mardon/GAP Interiors 9. Salvage Shutters

Instead of investing in expensive window treatments, consider using painted shutters for a charming and budget-friendly alternative. Louvered-top designs offer privacy while still allowing natural light to filter through. The adjustable slats also give you control over the direction of sunlight entering the room. Look for salvaged shutters at antique stores or architectural salvage yards for unique pieces that add character to your bathroom. A fresh coat of paint can easily update old shutters to match your bathroom’s color scheme. Photo by Caroline Mardon/GAP Interiors

Pro Tip: Mike Whiteside, co-owner of Black Dog Salvage in Roanoke, Virginia, notes that many old shutters were custom built for specific windows: “Each shutter was custom built for one window, so when you took it off to paint, or make repairs, you had to have a system of identifying which one went where.” Look for Roman numerals carved into the frame—a charming detail that adds to their salvage character. 10. Try a Mixed-Material Floor

Combine different textures and materials to add visual interest to your bathroom floor. For example, pair wood planks with pebble tile around the bathtub area to create an unexpected and eye-catching design. This approach adds a natural, spa-like element and allows you to use more expensive materials in smaller quantities, keeping costs down. The contrast between smooth wood and textured pebbles can create a sensory experience underfoot, bringing a touch of the outdoors inside. Photo by Caroline Mardon/GAP Interiors 11. Create a Beadboard Canopy

Elevate your bathroom’s design by adding a beadboard canopy to the ceiling. Extend the beadboard a few inches down the walls to create a cozy, enveloping effect. Paint the canopy a tranquil shade of sky blue to evoke the feeling of old-timey porch ceilings. This simple addition can make a significant impact on the room’s ambiance, adding visual interest overhead and making the space feel more intimate. The beadboard texture also adds a classic, cottage-like charm to your bathroom decor. Photo by Roger Davies

Pro Tip: Designer Jane Coslick, a cottage renovation specialist, recommends beaded plywood panels for this look: “The panels have 3-inch centers and look like individual boards. They mimic what you normally see on porch ceilings,” says Coslick. She painted hers one of her favorite blues, Benjamin Moore’s Tropicana Cabana, then added a colorful vintage mirror as a finishing touch 12. Choose a Checkerboard Floor

For a classic look that’s easy on the budget, consider installing a checkerboard floor using vinyl composition tile (VCT). This durable material offers an inexpensive alternative to ceramic tile while still providing a stylish, two-toned effect. To achieve the look of smaller squares without the added cost, use a vinyl tile cutter to slice 12-inch tiles into 4-inch pieces. This DIY approach allows you to create a custom pattern that fits your bathroom’s dimensions perfectly. VCT is also water-resistant and easy to maintain, making it an ideal choice for bathroom flooring. Photo by Nikki Crisp/IPC Images 13. Repurpose a Vintage Vanity

Transform a vintage mirrored dresser into a unique sink cabinet for a one-of-a-kind bathroom vanity. Start by making a cutout in the top of the dresser to accommodate a drop-in sink. Scout garage sales and flea markets for inexpensive dressers with character. You can choose to preserve the original finish for an authentic vintage look or refinish the piece to match your bathroom’s color scheme. Photo by Deborah Whitlaw Llewellyn 14. Carve a Curved backsplash

Give a wood vanity the appearance of a vintage washstand by adding a custom demilune backsplash. Instead of using wood, which can warp in the humid bathroom environment, opt for a sheet of marine-grade polymer. This material is easy to cut with a jigsaw and never needs painting, making it a low-maintenance choice. The curved shape adds a soft, elegant touch to the vanity and helps protect the wall from water splashes. This simple addition can elevate the look of a basic vanity, giving it a more refined and custom appearance. Photo by Deborah Whitlaw Llewellyn 15. Brighten the Existing Tile

Refresh your vintage tiled floor without the expense of replacement by using an eco-friendly acid etcher. This process removes ground-in dirt from both the tile surface and grout lines, revealing the original beauty of your floor. This method is particularly effective for enhancing and celebrating original design features in older homes. Not only does it revive the appearance of your bathroom, but it also extends the life of your existing tile, saving you money on costly replacements. Photo by Mick Frangou/IPC+ Syndication 16. Hang a Vintage Pendant

Add a touch of sophistication to your bathroom by hanging a chandelier in an unexpected spot, such as over the bathtub. A unique light fixture can draw attention to high ceilings, making the space feel larger and more luxurious. Look for rewired and ready-to-install chandeliers salvaged from old homes to find affordable, one-of-a-kind pieces. This simple update can dramatically change the ambiance of your bathroom, creating a focal point and adding a layer of elegance to the space. Photo by Laurey W. Glenn 17. Create a Harmonious Color Palette

Transform your bathroom with a fresh coat of paint to create a cohesive and striking color scheme. For a bold, monochromatic look, paint the walls to match your floors. Alternatively, try a two-tone approach by separating wall colors with a chair rail. Both options can create visual harmony and make your bathroom feel more put-together. When selecting colors, consider the existing elements in your bathroom, such as tile and fixtures, to ensure a complementary palette that enhances the overall aesthetic of the space. Photo by Aimee Herring 18. Install Whitewashed Barn Wood

Add character and warmth to your bathroom walls with whitewashed salvaged barn wood. This rustic-chic treatment refines the raw look of reclaimed wood while maintaining its natural texture and charm. The whitewashing process is simple and affordable, allowing you to achieve a high-end look on a budget. This technique works particularly well as an accent wall or behind a vanity, adding depth and interest to your bathroom design. Photo by Colin Poole/GAP Interiors 19. Make a Multi-Hued Mosaic

Create a custom floor design by personalizing plain sheets of white mesh-backed floor tile with colorful accents. Design a pattern by removing select white tiles and replacing them with colored ones of the same size and shape. To keep costs down, search for discounted or overstock tiles at tile showrooms. For as little as $10, you can purchase enough accent tiles to create a unique and eye-catching floor design. This DIY approach allows you to achieve a custom look without the high price tag of pre-designed mosaic tiles. Photo by John Granen 20. Wall-Mount a Cabinet

Install a wall-mounted cabinet to maximize space in a small bathroom. This sleek storage solution doesn’t take up valuable floor space, making it ideal for compact bathrooms. Choose a cabinet with a mirrored front to serve double duty as both storage and a vanity mirror. To further optimize storage, consider adding open shelving above or beside the cabinet. Conceal the Curtain Rod

Install a ceiling-mounted track to keep your shower curtain hardware out of sight. This clever design trick creates a cleaner look and makes the room appear taller by drawing the eye upward. To save on the cost of a custom curtain, consider making your own using burlap or a canvas drop cloth. These materials offer a natural, textured look that complements various bathroom styles. Ceiling-mount tracks are affordable, with many options starting at around $30 on curtain-tracks.com, making this an economical way to upgrade your shower area. Photo by Paul Costello/Otto 22. Try Red-Hot Linoleum

Make a bold statement with a vibrant linoleum floor. Sheet linoleum is an excellent choice for high-traffic areas like bathrooms, as it can withstand spills and splashes while remaining easy to clean. A bright red or other vivid color can instantly energize your space and create a focal point. Linoleum is also an eco-friendly and budget-conscious option, available in a wide range of colors and patterns to suit any design style. Photo by Jan Baldwin/IPC+ Syndication 23. Pursue a Period Piece

Place a freestanding tub on a diagonal to create the illusion of more space without costly renovations. This clever placement trick can make a small bathroom feel larger and more luxurious. Be sure to leave a few inches between the tub’s rim and the walls to enhance the spacious feel. When shopping for a tub, consider visiting salvage yards to find affordable, vintage options that can add character to your bathroom while keeping costs down. Photo by Laurey W. Glenn 24. Use Water-Resistant Wainscot

Install beadboard paneling made from cellular PVC for a classic look that stands up to moisture. This material is more affordable than most wall tiles and requires less maintenance than wood, making it an ideal choice for bathrooms. The water-resistant properties of PVC ensure that your wainscoting will look great for years to come without the need for painting or frequent repairs. This addition can add texture and visual interest to your bathroom walls while providing practical protection against splashes and humidity. Photo by Paul Whicheloe 25. Add a Decorative Window

Replace a plain glass window with a stained or leaded glass panel to enhance privacy and add character to your bathroom. This update allows natural light to filter through while obscuring the view from outside. If you can’t find an exact fit for your window opening, consider hanging a decorative panel from chains in front of the existing window. Photo by Laurey W. Glenn Our Conclusion
