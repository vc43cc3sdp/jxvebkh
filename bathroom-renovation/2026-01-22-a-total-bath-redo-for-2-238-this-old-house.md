Sometimes it takes an addition to the family to kick a remodel into high gear. Such was the case for Lisa and Jim Steele of Syracuse, New York. The couple was “skeeved out” by the grim lone full bath left behind by previous owners, and they dealt with the eyesore by taking quick showers—never baths.

As reported by Danielle Blundell in This Old House Magazine, the Steeles smartly offset demolition costs by selling the original copper pipes and the old claw-foot tub, bringing their gut-job debris removal expense down to zero. That resourcefulness set the tone for the entire project, where every dollar was carefully allocated across new fixtures and finishes. Before: Dark and Dingy

But with son Coby, now 3, on the way, they envisioned tub times full of toys for their little one. So, shopping sales and eBay for discounted fixtures, they began to create an inviting bath in their 1925 cottage, piece by piece.

Among their smartest savings moves: choosing ceramic tiles that mimic the look of real slate for easy-care flooring, and sourcing designer look-alike tiles from a big-box store for a vintage-inspired shower surround—giving the 1925 cottage period-appropriate style without the premium price tag.

Even with a tight budget, they scored a cast-iron tub on sale and hired a contractor only for the tasks that demanded professional skill—tiling the shower surround, installing drywall, and painting the walls—while handling the rest of the work themselves. Crisp and Clean Photo by David Lamb
