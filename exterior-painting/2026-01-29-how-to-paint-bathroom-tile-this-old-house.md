Choosing the Right Paint for Bathroom Tiles

For the best results, use epoxy or acrylic paint specifically designed for bathroom tiles. These types of paints are durable, water-resistant, and can handle the frequent cleaning and moisture that bathrooms endure. Epoxy acrylic paint, in particular, adheres well to tile surfaces and offers the longevity needed for a high-moisture environment.

Even when using an epoxy coating specifically designed for tile, proper adhesion requires etching the tile surface. In a This Old House project, the team used a two-step etching process: first scrubbing with a calcium rust cleaner on abrasive pads, followed by a second etching pass over the entire surface. Before any etching, the tile was thoroughly cleaned with a bleach and water mix and scrubbed with a scouring pad, paying extra attention to grease-prone areas. Preparation: The Key to Success

Step-by-Step Guide to Painting Bathroom Tiles

Follow these steps to transform your bathroom tiles: 1. Remove Obstacles

Begin by removing any wall plates, fasteners, or hardware that could get in the way. 2. Clean and Prepare the Tile Surface

Apply painter’s tape to the edges, fixtures, and walls to keep paint from getting where it doesn’t belong. Cover sinks, toilets, and floors with plastic drop cloths or resin paper to protect them from dust and paint drips. 4. Sand the Tiles

As This Old House general contractor Tommy Silva puts it: “Sanding is the key to a good finish. If you don’t have a good sandy job, the finish isn’t gonna fill in.” Take your time with this step—rushing through sanding is one of the most common mistakes in any painting project.

Sanding creates a slightly rough surface that helps the paint adhere better. Using 320-grit sandpaper, sand the surface of the tiles to create a rough texture that will help the paint adhere. For larger areas, a random orbital sander can save time. Don’t forget to sand grout lines as well. 5. Wipe Down and Vacuum Dust

After sanding, wipe the tiles with a microfiber cloth to remove dust. Then, use a wet-dry vac with a HEPA filter to clean up any remaining debris.

Pro Tip: Painter John Dee, who has worked on three This Old House project houses, makes re-dusting a habit before every coat: “I always re-dust the section I’m about to paint, no matter how many times it’s been dusted before. It’s a reflex.” Even if you’ve already wiped down and vacuumed, give the surface one more pass with a soft brush right before you apply primer. 6. Apply the Paint

Mix your epoxy acrylic paint according to the manufacturer’s directions. Begin by cutting in around the edges with a paintbrush. Then, use a foam roller to apply the paint in small sections, working in W-shaped strokes to prevent lap marks.

For the best coverage, especially over grout lines, follow up with a microfiber roller for the second coat.

Pro Tip: In a This Old House project updating a dated kitchen backsplash, the team emphasized the importance of maintaining a wet edge while painting tile. As the pro advised the homeowner: “Just don’t get too far ahead of me because we always want a wet edge.” The two-coat process doesn’t require heavy application on the first pass—you want to make sure the paint doesn’t drip. Then, on the second coat, focus on keeping the paint “a little bit tighter to the grout line.” 7. Let It Dry

Allow the first coat to dry according to the paint manufacturer’s instructions, usually around 6–8 hours. After the second coat, let the tiles cure for the recommended time before using the bathroom, typically 48–72 hours.

Tips for a Professional Paint Finish on Tile

To achieve the best results when painting your bathroom tiles, follow these tips:

Allow adequate drying time between coats and before using the bathroom.

Apply thin, even coats rather than one thick coat to prevent drips and achieve proper drying.

Regularly maintain the painted surface by cleaning gently and avoiding abrasive cleaners.

Use a clear topcoat for added durability in high-moisture areas.

Work in a well-ventilated area and wear appropriate safety gear, including a respirator.
